Transparency about how generative AI may support the development and delivery of this course

Faculty-directed. AI-assisted. Human-reviewed.

How This Course Was Developed

Portions of this course were developed with the assistance of generative artificial intelligence. The course’s academic content, learning objectives, and instructional direction were provided by the faculty member and/or academic department. AI tools may have assisted with activities such as drafting, organizing content, developing examples, creating visuals, or preparing instructional materials. All AI-assisted material was reviewed and approved before being made available to students. The faculty member and academic department remain responsible for the accuracy, quality, and appropriateness of the final course content.

How Faculty AI Use Differs from Student AI Use

Faculty and students may use generative AI for different purposes and under different expectations. Faculty may use AI to support course development, instruction, and administrative work while remaining professionally responsible for reviewing its output and making final decisions.

Student work is used to demonstrate learning and progress toward the course objectives. Whether students may use generative AI—and how they may use it—therefore depends on what an activity is intended to assess. AI use may be encouraged when it supports the learning goals, limited when students need to practice particular skills independently, or prohibited when it would prevent the instructor from accurately evaluating a student’s own knowledge or abilities.

Faculty use of AI does not automatically mean that students may use AI for every assignment. Students should follow the AI-use expectations provided in this syllabus and for each course activity, when applicable. When expectations are unclear, students should ask the instructor before using an AI tool.

When AI Is Used to Review and Assess Student Work

Faculty may use approved AI tools to assist with portions of the review process, such as organizing observations, comparing work with a rubric, identifying areas that may need additional attention, or drafting possible feedback. AI-generated observations and feedback are reviewed by the faculty member before they are used.

AI does not replace the faculty member’s academic judgment or responsibility for evaluating student work. The faculty member determines the final grade and is responsible for ensuring that feedback and grading decisions are appropriate, accurate, and consistent with the assignment criteria. Student work will be handled in accordance with applicable university requirements for privacy, security, and responsible technology use.
