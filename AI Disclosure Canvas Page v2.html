Transparency about how generative AI supports the development and delivery of this course

Faculty-directed. AI-assisted. Human-reviewed.

Generative AI refers to tools that create or transform text, images, audio, video, code, or other content in response to instructions. This page explains where those tools may be used, the safeguards that apply, and who remains responsible for the work.

How This Course Was Developed and Reviewed

The course’s academic content, learning objectives, assessment standards, and instructional direction were established by the faculty member and/or academic department. Generative AI may have supported parts of the development process, but it did not independently determine what students should learn or how their learning should be evaluated.

Before AI-assisted materials are made available to students, the faculty member or an appropriate content expert reviews and, when needed, revises them for:

  • factual and disciplinary accuracy;
  • alignment with the course learning objectives and assessment criteria;
  • clarity, relevance, accessibility, and appropriate sourcing; and
  • potential bias, exclusion, or misleading information.

The faculty member and academic department remain responsible for the accuracy, quality, appropriateness, and instructional use of the final course content.

How Faculty AI Use Differs from Student AI Use

Faculty and students are both expected to use generative AI transparently, responsibly, and in ways consistent with university requirements. Their permitted uses may differ because their roles and the purposes of their work are different.

Faculty may use AI to support course development, instruction, and administrative work while remaining professionally responsible for reviewing its output and making final academic decisions. Student work is used to demonstrate learning and progress toward course objectives. Whether students may use generative AI—and how they may use it—therefore depends on what an activity is intended to assess.

AI use may be encouraged when it supports the learning goals, limited when students need to practice particular skills independently, or prohibited when it would prevent the instructor from accurately evaluating a student’s own knowledge or abilities. Faculty use of AI does not automatically mean that students may use AI for every assignment. Students should follow the AI-use expectations provided in this syllabus and for each course activity, when applicable. When expectations are unclear, students should ask the instructor before using an AI tool.

When AI Is Used to Review and Assess Student Work

Faculty may use an ASU-approved AI tool to assist with limited parts of the review process, such as organizing observations, comparing work with a faculty-developed rubric, identifying areas that may need closer attention, or drafting possible feedback. The AI tool does not replace the instructor’s reading of the work or the instructor’s academic judgment.

The faculty member reviews AI-assisted observations and feedback for accuracy, relevance, potential bias, and consistency with the assignment criteria before they are used. AI does not independently determine a student’s final grade, and an AI-generated result will not be the sole basis for an academic integrity finding. The faculty member remains responsible for all feedback, grading decisions, and academic integrity determinations.

Protecting Student Work and Information

Student work or personally identifiable information will not be entered into public or personal AI accounts for course review or assessment. When AI assistance involves student work, faculty will use only institutionally approved tools and follow applicable ASU requirements for privacy, security, intellectual property, and responsible technology use. ASU-provisioned AI tools include contractual data protections that prohibit vendors from using ASU prompts or data to train their models.

Questions, Concerns, and Human Review

Students may ask the instructor how generative AI was used in a course resource, feedback, or assessment process. If a student believes that feedback or a grade does not accurately reflect the submitted work or the stated criteria, the student should contact the instructor and may use the course’s usual review or appeal process. The use of AI does not reduce a student’s opportunity to receive an explanation or request human review.

If the way AI is used in this course changes materially, students will be informed before the change affects submitted work, feedback, or grading.
